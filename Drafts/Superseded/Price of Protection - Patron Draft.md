## Scene 2 — Stop the Presses: aftermath, not combat (0:05–0:20)

**The raid has already happened.** It took place while the heroes were occupied at the factory, against an investigation that predated their involvement. Do not play a second attack, roll initiative here, or hide an ambush inside an optional test. The meaningful choices concern testimony, protection, and what the heroes do next.

### People at the annex

- **Mara Doss — INVENTED; Font commissioning editor; not previously met.** Human, spectacles repaired with wire, ink under her nails. Wants a defensible story and living sources. Direct, curious, quick to correct an error. She will not fabricate quotes or publish a PC's private history without permission.

>
> *At the proofing table, a woman in wire-mended spectacles presses a folded apron against a cut above her eye.*

Let the heroes choose where they put their attention: Mara, Beren, the wrecked workspace, or the marked bundles. Asking sensible questions reveals the facts without a roll.

> *“The clerk's name is Tovin. He's missing too.”*

If the heroes have not already described the factory, Mara shows them the address on Corbin's copy:

> *“The Ministry paid for work at this factory, but it's listed as closed. Have you been there? Can you tell me what was happening inside?”*

Give the heroes room to say what they found, challenge her proposed account, request anonymity, or refuse to expose private details. Mara separates eyewitness observations from guesses about who is behind the payments. Neither she nor Melvin knew about Kell until told; neither has identified the person directing the wider operation.

### Three useful facts — automatic, with concrete sources

1. **Mara's proof and Corbin's copy:** Ministry grant → Civic Loomworks and Festooning → factory expenses. That is financial support for a site, not proof of magical murder.
2. **Beren's account:** Plain-coated bravos with mixed weapons, no Ministry uniforms or warrant. Their covered cart bore a Rasp **South Shuttle** booking tag.
3. **A Font dispatch:** Tovin has not returned; another office confirms Civic Loomworks has an active booking at that depot. The Font sends a courier to the Rasp, which begins checking it.

If the players distrust testimony, show the booking tag copied into Beren's ordinary delivery tally. There is no cipher, mandatory search roll, or chance to lose the sole lead. The attackers wanted this investigation stopped; they were not kidnapping every witness to the amphitheater.

### Optional interactions, not an obstacle course

Comfort a source, help Beren, carry intact material to the binding room, or help distinguish attribution from rumor. These ordinarily just work. A relevant heroic ability can help in its stated way; do not declare that inspecting an invoice establishes the Author's nature or that psionics automatically reads a person's memories.

For uncertain extra assistance, one easy Reason or Intuition test with an applicable skill: ≤11 help succeeds but consumes supplies or several minutes; 12–16 succeeds; 17+ succeeds and a source provides a practical detail about the cart; natural 19–20 adds the next useful contact. No result withholds a core fact, causes a new fight, or heals the heroes automatically. No Victory is awarded for this scene.

A courier and two guards secure the annex, transport injured staff, warn other offices, and arrange printing elsewhere. Mara can join the following conversation in the protected binding room. The institution is acting on several fronts while the heroes consider one urgent recovery job. The party is not asked to single-handedly save the entire Font.

If the heroes propose going straight to the depot, Tessa's briefing and Sera's conversation can happen along the way or at the staging point. Offer all three conversations; do not force the heroes to sit through them if they deliberately decline.

## Scene 3 — Who Can Protect You? (0:20–1:05)

Mara stays under Font protection while Ily and Tovin are missing. Eda Pell, a one-shot duty editor, can speak for the Font if an actual player-driven event makes Mara unavailable; do not engineer another attack.

Melvin can explain jurisdiction from his experience: he was turned away by a different district's officers; his badge does not command their guards. He can seek Sera's help through an ordinary messenger. Neither source lectures about the entire constitution.

### Three conversations, three choices

Capital still has seven major powers; only **the Font, the Rasp, and House Vorona** offer retainers in this session. Alvaro, Navarr, Valetta, and the Fulcrum remain background institutions, not a second menu. Explain them only if a question calls for it. The two guilds bought Great House status; the Font and Rasp are political peers, not businesses subordinate to Vorona.

**Forty-five-minute allocation:** Fifteen minutes with each patron. Use roughly three minutes to establish what they have done and can offer, eight minutes for player questions and bargaining, and four minutes to test a concrete disagreement or boundary. Protect all three conversations; the final fifteen-minute decision is additional time, not taken from these scenes. No representative is replaced by a letter.

The Font is already involved. Its courier contacts the Rasp about the booking; Tessa speaks to the party after checking it. Melvin requests a meeting with his marine contact, Sera. This is normal coordination between working agents, not three rulers appearing because Kell died. Narrate ordinary travel; no day-long break is needed. If the heroes refuse a particular meeting, respect that refusal, but do not cut a conversation merely for pacing.

### Conversation 1 — Mara Doss, the Font: what goes into print?

**Established power:** Inān al-Adwiyya's broadsheets and Paperfeathers carry news and information across Capital. **Proposed handler:** Mara, a commissioning editor authorized to retain sources/recovery agents on a modest local budget; Eda Pell serves in her place only if an actual player-driven event makes Mara unavailable.

Mara places the surviving proof beside the incident report. Ask the players which statements they will put their names to, and which sources must remain anonymous.

> *“May I name you as witnesses in the story? You can stay anonymous, but named witnesses make the report harder to dismiss. Either way, I need to know what you saw yourselves and what someone else told you.”*

She accepts accurate anonymous testimony but explains its weaker public force. She corrects any demonstrated error immediately; she does not insist on revealing private backstory. On an outcome with lost originals, she offers an attributed account until new corroboration is recovered.

**Offer:** Safe lodging, reporting contacts, an accurate public account, and ordinary agreed mission expenses. **Initial obligation:** Recover living sources and corroborating records. **Price:** First publication rights and no promise to suppress accurate, relevant facts merely because they embarrass an ally. **Boundary:** No invented quotes or unwanted exposure of vulnerable sources; no command over another faction's guards.

### Conversation 2 — Tessa Rook, the Rasp: who gets the people and vehicles back?

**Established power:** Lord Kashimir's transport network, imported tapestries, and Kites couriers. **Proposed handler:** Tessa, a depot overseer authorized to hire recovery help, not to rewrite guild policy.

Tessa sets aside the false cargo booking and asks what the heroes will prioritize if they cannot save every vehicle and person. She favors people, including her workers; she accepts damaged carpets if damage was necessary to prevent abduction. Her answer makes the assets-versus-lives choice explicit without inventing a compulsory dilemma in the fight.

> *“Free the prisoners and get our workers out. Save the carpets if you can, but break one if that's what it takes to stop them carrying someone off.”*

**Offer:** Safe transit, a courier contact, depot access, protected lodging on a guild route, and ordinary agreed mission expenses. **Initial obligation:** Recover workers, vehicles, and the booking book. **Price:** Return guild assets and give the Rasp first access to discoveries about its routes and personnel. **Boundary:** Tessa cannot buy silence about proven crimes or claim custody of every prisoner. She wants a specific corrupt manager exposed, not his guilt assumed to extend to every worker.

This establishes her identity and the booking fraud; Scene 4 is only the five-minute tactical briefing, not a fourth patron conversation.

### Conversation 3 — Captain Sera Marche, House Vorona: whose protection is visible?

**Established power:** Duke Marco Vorona's navy, Far Mariners, military academy, and blackpowder. **Proposed handler:** Sera, a working captain with the limited resources described here. Melvin makes the introduction; she is not automatically an old friend or previously met.

Sera asks for the shortest version of the incident that the heroes can personally attest to. She offers escorted refuge inside Vorona territory, then asks whether they will publicly acknowledge whose protection they accepted.

> *“I can give you rooms and guards in Vorona territory. In another house's district, their officers can refuse to let my soldiers in. In return for our protection, I want you to tell people that the Far Mariners are backing you.”*

Her soldiers protect evacuees and routes off-map, not take over the party's encounter. Accepting the offer strengthens Vorona's case for wider policing; it does not immediately award the house jurisdiction over the annex or depot.

**Offer:** Secure lodging, a military contact, operational introductions, and ordinary agreed mission expenses. **Initial obligation:** Bring a defensible account of Rusk's operation and deliver rescued people to agreed safety. **Price:** Public association with the Far Mariners and an expectation of support for their civic role. **Boundary:** No universal arrest authority, no order to endorse an unlawful occupation, and no use of civilians as bait.

### Give the conversations room to develop

Use the players' questions first. The prompts below are fallback material, not a checklist or secret test.

| Patron | Question that makes the offer concrete | Honest answer to the hard question | Negotiable concession | Real refusal boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Font | “Which events did you witness yourselves, and may I name you when I report them?” | Mara cannot guarantee publicity will protect every source; she can correct the record and keep promises about anonymity | Hold publication until captives are safe; agree a specific review window for factual corrections | Fabricating evidence, naming a protected source without consent, or giving the party a permanent veto over accurate reporting |

| Vorona | “Would you publicly support Far Mariner patrols in more districts, or would you only give us credit for the help we've given you?” | Sera's backing helps Vorona's influence; rivals may regard the party differently, and her uniform does not grant citywide authority | Credit only assistance actually provided; preserve the party's independent name and right to refuse unlawful orders | Claiming naval orders that were never given, using civilians as bait, or promising an armed occupation she cannot authorize |

**Each conversation should leave something to compare:** ask the party to state one thing they value about the offer and one condition they want written down. A handler can disagree without becoming a villain. Make refusals specific and explain what help remains available.

**Information stays bounded:** Mara can discuss journalism and source protection; Tessa can discuss actual bookings and workers; Sera can discuss her own command and jurisdiction. None knows every rival's intentions, the Author's identity, or a new fact about Demona's disgrace. If asked about the other four powers, give a sentence of established institutional context and return to these three real offers; do not open new patron auditions.

None of these conversations identifies Dury as a suspect. If players independently ask who heads the Ministry, answer the public-office question honestly, but distinguish that fact from evidence of involvement. Do not prompt the question, propose him as the culprit, or treat the party's own hypothesis as a confirmed revelation.

**Roleplay changes the eventual agreement:** Record provisional terms now. At the coda, the handler responds to what the heroes actually recovered and which promises they kept. Rescuing people without the original papers still matters; a damaged carpet does not erase a successful rescue. No hidden points, correct dialogue sequence, or compulsory negotiation test.

## Scene 4 — The Rasp's Missing Cargo (1:05–1:10)

**Tessa Rook — INVENTED; Rasp depot overseer, not previously met.** Human, leather dispatch wallet, chalk on her cuffs. She speaks plainly, counts people before vehicles, and has authority to hire local recovery help. She has not known all along about Rusk's abductions.

>
> *“Civic Loomworks hired two cargo carpets at South Shuttle Depot. The booking says they're carrying festival screens. No passengers.” She looks at the account of the covered cart. “If that cart took Ily there, someone at the depot is hiding prisoners in a cargo shipment.”*

Nearby guards protect alternate routes and receive evacuees. They do not appear as extra combatants. Disabling incoming traffic stops reinforcements, not the booked outgoing carpets already under Rusk's control. There is no remote “turn every carpet off” power.

**Three approaches, choose one; no separate challenge:**

An alternative that actually solves the problem works. No required cipher, guessed password, or multi-stage machine puzzle.

**Rusk — INVENTED; Ministry enforcement captain acting through a private contractor.** Closely cropped grey hair, polished boots above a dirty hem, bloodstone cuff. ### Objects, hostages, and departures — scenario rules

**Prisoners:** No monster rolls strikes against them. The enclosure has a gate with Stamina 15, stability 3, no immunity; destroy it with damage, take Olan's key after he surrenders, or spend a main action adjacent to pick the ordinary lock without a roll. Olan surrenders when a hero reaches him and offers protection. Free prisoners move 3 squares per round at round end toward (2,2), avoiding enemy-adjacent spaces. An adjacent hero can instead lead one 3 squares as a maneuver once per round. Crossing the escape door removes them safely. Enemy forces do not get free off-map hostage executions.

**Shutters:** Each lever closes or opens its labeled bay shutter as a maneuver. A creature adjacent to the closed shutter can spend a main action jamming it with a crate; thereafter reopening needs a main action at that shutter to clear it and a maneuver at the lever. The shutter itself has Stamina 30 and stability 4. These actions do not require a puzzle or test. Allies and enemies can use them, on their own turns.

**Carpets:** Two commercial platforms, each 5 × 4 squares, hover 1 square above the floor. Friendly or hostile crew members are separate from the vehicle. An adjacent creature boards as normal movement with 1 extra square to step up. A creature aboard can use a move action to pilot it up to 3 squares horizontally within the building or through its open bay; all riders move with it, but this does not grant riders extra actions. Only one pilot move per carpet each round. Attackers cannot use the departure event for an extra mid-round attack. Each carpet has Stamina 25, stability 2, no immunity; disabling one drops it its single square without fall damage and prevents departure. Rasp accepts necessary damage to save people.

**Bale:** A main action at its release drops it into the marked 2 × 2 space; creatures there suffer 6 damage and fall prone. Everyone can see its footprint. It remains as movement-blocking cover. This neutral, one-use prop has no enemy-only activation and adds no EV.

**Round-5 departure:** Show two tokens labeled A/B and say when the bell will ring. At the **end of round 5**, check each vehicle once: at least one willing crew member aboard, carpet functional, shutter open, and a path clear for its entire 5-by-4-square footprint. If all conditions hold, it leaves with whoever and whatever is physically aboard. A hostile crew counts as an escaped abduction team only if at least one enemy departs. Friendly commandeered craft do not commit the planned abduction. Cancelled, crewless, destroyed, or blocked craft do not depart. Do not override a successful blockade. No new crew spawns.

After the bell, launched vehicles leave this battle map; later pursuit is a follow-up scene, not a surprise second combat. Remaining crews try to flee on foot instead of starting a new assault. Rusk must use ordinary movement to reach a vehicle or exit; he is not extracted automatically.

**Ending:** Rusk at 0 Stamina, captured, or fully surrendered ends organized resistance immediately; surviving hired crews put weapons down or flee. Finish freeing people without attack rolls. If he escapes, organized ground resistance also dissolves, but his operation is only partially broken. A blockade of both carpets alone prevents tonight's outgoing jobs, but does not automatically defeat a healthy Rusk: the heroes may finish him, secure his surrender, or take that partial win and withdraw.

**Rewards:** 2 Victories for neutralizing Rusk and securing the depot, by force or a genuine alternate solution. No extra Victory for prisoners or carpets. If he remains at large, record partial success without the 2-Victory award. Maximum session reward is 2.

**Evidence:** The pay chest contains the press-raid instruction, a contractor payment record matching Mara's invoice, and the list of intended abduction targets. Taking a portable chest or its papers is a maneuver adjacent, no test. Rusk carries a key but the unlocked pay drawer can simply be opened. If these papers are destroyed or lost, Tovin and surviving captives still give usable testimony; publication and prosecution are weaker, not impossible. Neither documents nor testimony reveal the Author's body/location or explain Dorian.

### Rules at this battlefield

- **Initiative:** Heroes and enemy groups alternate turns; heroes choose their own order. Every creature acts once per round. A group resolves all its members together. No allies add combat turns or encounter strength; their help is off-map.
- **Malice:** Start this encounter with the party's average current Victories (round down). At the start of each round, add living heroes plus round number: normally +5, +6, +7, +8, +9. Discard unspent Malice after this encounter. Use only the printed abilities and Human Malice options below; there are no hidden reinforcements.
- **Potency and duration:** M/A/R/I/P refer to Might/Agility/Reason/Intuition/Presence; a condition after “M < 2,” for example, applies only if Might is below 2. EoT ends at the end of the target's next turn, or its current turn if imposed during that turn. Save ends: roll d10 at each turn's end; 6+ ends the effect.
- **Relevant conditions:** Taunted gives a double bane on ability rolls that do not target the taunter while they are within line of effect. Slowed caps speed at 2 and prevents shifting. Restrained sets speed to 0, prevents Stand Up and forced movement, gives a bane on ability rolls and Might/Agility tests, and grants abilities against the creature an edge; teleporting ends it. Prone gives a bane on strikes, grants melee strikes against the creature an edge, and makes crawling cost 1 extra square; Stand Up is a maneuver. Grabbed sets speed to 0, prevents Knockback and forced movement except by its source, and gives a bane on abilities not targeting the grabber. The source brings its captive when it moves; its speed is halved if the captive is at least its size. The grab ends if released, the target teleports, or forced movement separates the pair. Conditions do not stack with themselves.
- **Recovery:** Catch Breath is a maneuver: spend one of the hero's own Recoveries to regain their recovery value in Stamina. A dying hero cannot Catch Breath. There is no automatic healing between scenes.
- **Free strikes:** These monsters deal their listed fixed Free Strike damage, without a roll; range is their signature's range or melee 1/ranged 5, whichever is greater. An opportunity attack is a melee free strike when an adjacent enemy willingly moves away without shifting or teleporting. Forced movement does not provoke. A monster with a bane against that target cannot make the opportunity attack.
- **Positioning:** Cover exists when a solid object blocks at least half the target and imposes a bane on damage-dealing abilities against it. Difficult terrain costs 1 extra square to enter. Stability reduces horizontal forced movement by its value, minimum 0. Falling 2+ squares deals 2 damage per square and leaves the creature prone; reduce effective height by Agility, minimum 0. Use stairs and ladders at 1 extra square of movement per square climbed unless the creature has a climb speed.
- **Captures:** A hero reducing a creature to 0 Stamina can knock it unconscious rather than kill it. Do not require lethal damage to end an encounter. Do not attack downed heroes for convenience.

**Minions, fully inline:** Each color is a separate squad of four with its own pool. All members share one signature roll when attacking. Each target takes one attack result; a second or third minion on that same target adds its Free Strike damage (at most three attackers per target). Each minion has a move and a main action, a move and a maneuver, or two moves—not all three. A minion using a personal maneuver does not join the squad attack. A critical hit gives each participating minion a free strike.

Reduce a squad's pool by damage suffered. At every multiple of individual Stamina lost, remove one minion. A non-area hit can remove additional nearest squad members after the struck one; let the player narrate it. An area ability damages the pool once per affected minion, but cannot kill minions outside its area; cap that area damage at individual Stamina times affected members before immunity/weakness. Apply a squad's immunity or weakness only once to each damage source, not once per affected minion. Minions cannot regain or gain temporary Stamina and cannot be winded. Simultaneous free strikes from one squad into one target are one strike with summed damage.

Attach the named captain to its colored squad; keep the captain's Stamina separate. While that captain lives, the squad gains its printed With Captain bonus. A captain leads only one squad. A replacement non-minion can take command at the start of the next round. All squad members and their captain activate in the same initiative group.

**Human features below are book rules, not cult evidence.** Supernatural Insight ignores supernatural concealment; it does not read minds, detect lies, or defeat mundane cover. No monster gains additional unprinted abilities.

Defeat, capture, or unconditional surrender of Rusk immediately ends organized enemy resistance.
