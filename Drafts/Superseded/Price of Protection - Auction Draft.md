## Scene 2 — A Story Still Being Written (0:10–0:30)

**Ottilie Farrow:** Human commissioning editor, wire-repaired spectacles, ink on her fingers. Curious and precise, willing to change her draft when corrected. She has approval to draw on the Font's special-investigations fund for this commission, not promise protection across Capital. **Ily**, her adult apprentice (23), is working normally. **Beren**, the adult dwarf typesetter, is setting another story. Ottilie corrects her draft from their firsthand account. Her original investigation concerned the payments, not a guarded entrance. The boarding-up crew is a new development reported this morning; she does not claim it was there yesterday or assume its workers belong to the cult.

### A paid job, not an allegiance

> *“Find out who sent that crew and what those earlier payments bought. Bring back the written orders and the bills, or someone who can show us where to find them. I need to know we can prove what we print.”*

**The larger commission:** Deliver a publishable account connecting the Ministry payment record, the earlier factory deliveries, and today's sealing order, with evidence Ottilie can independently verify. This is **S + F**; the later rescue and confrontation are separate from the reporting fee. Relevant copies or corroborated, privately verified testimony are sufficient; unrelated payroll stays private. Agree to the Font's use of substantiated facts for this investigation, with source anonymity and personal attribution negotiated separately. No upstream culprit, forced confession, public naming of a source, or proof of Vesk's killer is required.

**Why the Font pays this much:** Its editors have approved a major commission to turn an unverified payment into defensible reporting about Ministry-funded activity at a supposedly closed factory. The heroes are obtaining nonpublic evidence and taking the personal risk of challenging a protected operation. The money comes from the guild's investigative fund, not Ottilie's ordinary newsroom expenses. Her reporters obtain public records and check dates; the heroes handle the fieldwork. They are paid for reliable findings, not a predetermined accusation or how many enemies they fight.

> *Ottilie takes out a contract, writes a sum beside each of your names, and turns it toward you.*
>
> *“That's for each of you. Half when you show me who sent those workers, half when you show me where the earlier payments went. This is one job. You don't owe us the next one.”*

**Tell the players plainly:** The figure represents a substantial payment that will **increase each hero's Wealth score by 1** on full completion. Do not make them guess its value from an invented coin total.

**Prepared compensation:** **S + F verified and delivered = +1 Wealth to each hero**, once for this commission, in addition to the investigation Victory and any encounter reward. This is **one increase per hero, not one point divided among the party**; add it to each hero's actual Wealth rather than setting a common score. The payment is theirs to spend, not a loan, lodging allowance, or conditional patron benefit. If the heroes need to hire transport or rent a disguise, Ottilie can pay for that separately; agree on those purchases before they spend the money.

**Partial payment:** One verified finding earns half the monetary fee, but no investigation Victory yet and **no fractional Wealth score or Wealth increase yet**. Record the paid installment; if the missing finding is completed later, pay the remaining balance and award the single investigation Victory and +1 Wealth increase then. Do not award it twice. With neither finding verified, reimburse agreed expenses but pay no success fee. This is the commission's scenario reward structure, not a general coin-to-Wealth conversion. Prepare these awards now; record them at the table only after they are earned. Do not edit character files or the app.

Agree on what must stay private, which purchases the Font will pay for, whose names may appear in the paper, and what Ottilie may publish. She needs permission to report facts the heroes can prove, not ownership of every future discovery or a promise to work only for her. She will keep sources' names private if agreed and correct factual mistakes, but will not invent quotes or promise to blame someone before seeing the evidence.

The Font's bought Great House status means working with Ottilie can later open institutional doors. She is offering an introduction through useful work, not announcing seven recruiters. If asked, she can explain that reputation with her newsroom may lead to future introductions; none is promised or required today.

## Scene 4 — The Private Sale (0:55–1:20)

### A name in the wrong place

Anselmo waits behind the stage curtains while Belmonte meets the company paymaster upstairs. The clerk has been told to sign one more paper before being paid. The three scenery workers from the moon are delivering props beside the office; they see the payoff and hear the demand for the records. Belmonte keeps them waiting for their wages so he can force them to support his account too.

Anselmo can be approached privately, watched from the gallery, or reached while the party helps the workers. Put the troubling document in reach of their chosen method: he can show it, they can look over his shoulder, or they can read its duplicate on Belmonte's desk.

> *Anselmo turns a page back and reads it again. His thumb has worn a crease into the corner.*
>
> *“This says I kept the repair money.” He taps his printed name. “He told me it was a receipt.”*

The admission is **unsigned and false**. He handled the payments; the records show the money went to suppliers and Belmonte's crew, not his personal account. His worry is grounded in what the paper says, not a supernatural premonition.

> *“I'll show you the bills. But my name stays out of the paper, and I'm not going back upstairs alone.”*

Showing Ottilie's payment record, protecting his identity, and arranging a safe place to meet her can win his cooperation **without a compulsory test**. He allows copies of the factory pages, not unrelated workers' wages. Do not require a kidnapping or force him back into danger after the heroes secure his safety.

An uncertain appeal uses Presence/Reason and an appropriate skill: **17+** gains trust and copies now; **12–16** requires one stated reassurance, such as agreeing where he will wait or letting him supervise the copying; **≤11** leaves him unwilling to hand over the wallet yet, but he still shows the false admission and points out the office. Polite refusal does not make him an enemy. Change the offer or use another source of evidence, rather than rolling the same request repeatedly.

### Hear the deal

A conversation in the office can be overheard from an open gallery window, witnessed under a cover, or reported by Anselmo, who was present for the first exchange. Access determines how directly the heroes witness it, not whether they can learn the plan.

> *The paymaster puts the purse on the desk. Belmonte lays a hand on it.*
>
> *“The originals,” the paymaster says. “All of them.”*
>
> *“The clerk has the last pages. You'll get them when the sale closes.”*

**What is actually happening:** Civic Loomworks wants the records back after the factory disaster. Belmonte has demanded a private payoff for handing them over. He intends to have Anselmo's false admission survive while the genuine bills disappear. This is blackmail within the operation, not a Great House auction for the Author's identity. The paymaster is a company employee and can identify the company's ordinary office, not an unseen mastermind.

Demona can explain, without a roll, that **a payment to the captain personally does not purchase public records or authorize him to imprison a clerk**. She can use this distinction to challenge the paymaster, persuade staff not to help, or convince Anselmo that signing will not protect him. It is professional knowledge, not a revelation about her own past.

**Put the threat in plain sight before the fight.** Belmonte tells his sergeant within earshot of the office or gallery:

> *“When the buyers leave, shut the loading doors. Bring me Fenn. The workers stay too. Nobody leaves until I've got those papers.”*

The sergeant takes cord to the bays. A marksman carries a brazier from the prop display toward the desk. The workers are ordered to wait for their pay. Anselmo can be moved to safety now; the guards do not magically find him elsewhere.

### Plan the intervention

**The closing bell is the attack signal, not a detection result.** Belmonte has ordered the armed seizure and destruction of evidence whether or not anyone noticed the heroes. The heroes now have a concrete reason to intervene. Give them the last five minutes of this scene to choose where and how.

Record the benefits they actually earned; honor equivalent clever ideas:

- **Trusted clerk:** Copies of **F**, Anselmo safely out of the room, and the location of the duplicate ledger. No recapture or loss of those copies by fiat.
- **Helpful workers:** The side door is unlatched; bystanders clear the public aisle; the party knows which lever operates each shutter.
- **Convincing cover:** Heroes can begin on the gallery or beside a carpet, not all at the front gate. A successful specific diversion can remove or delay a real guard; do not respawn a replacement to restore the budget.
- **Political pressure or a witnessed bargain:** The paymaster may leave the purse, identify the office that sent them, or allow a receipt to be copied. That is more evidence and less cover for Belmonte, not a forced confession naming a minister.
- **Direct challenge:** Heroes can announce that the workers are leaving and confront Belmonte openly. Start the same encounter when he orders his armed crew to stop them.

Information gathering can succeed quietly; **the confrontation is its own planned scene**. Do not secretly downgrade successful preparation to manufacture surprise. If players deliberately abandon the intervention, record the resulting harm instead of teleporting enemies after them.

**Civilian procedure — scenario rule:** Anselmo and the workers each have speed 5, Stamina 10, and characteristics 0; they make no attacks and add no allied combat turns. At each round's end, a free civilian moves up to 5 squares toward a safe exit, using normal terrain and movement restrictions. A hero can direct them without an action. An enemy can spend a main action adjacent to bind a civilian with cord, preventing their own movement, but cannot do so while a conscious hero is adjacent to that civilian. The opening speech grants no free captures. A hero adjacent to a bound civilian can free them as a maneuver. Guards aim to capture, not execute; civilians reduced to 0 Stamina are unconscious and need carrying, not automatically dead. Picking up a willing, bound, or unconscious civilian is a maneuver adjacent, needs a free arm, and halves the carrier's speed; the civilian moves with the carrier and can be put down as a maneuver. Evacuated people do not reappear as hostages. These binding and carrying procedures apply only to these noncombatants, not to restraining heroes or monsters.

**Evidence:** Taking a paper bundle or the wallet is a maneuver adjacent; copying the relevant pages from an open ledger takes a main action adjacent. The office holds a routine duplicate, not magically unique evidence. Saved copies stay saved. **Burning a bundle requires an enemy main action adjacent to the brazier. It is destroyed at the end of the following round**, allowing a full round to retrieve it as a maneuver adjacent. Track the actual bundle: the original S order, F bills, or false admission. Never burn copies already elsewhere. Witnesses can still verify surviving facts.

**Shutters and flying carpets — scenario terrain:** Operate a lever as a maneuver. Jam a closed shutter with a crate as a main action adjacent; clearing it takes a main action, then someone must use the lever. Shutter Stamina 30, stability 4. Each 5 × 4 carpet has Stamina 25, stability 2 and hovers 1 square high; a rider can spend a move action to pilot it 3 squares, once per carpet per round. Passages must fit the entire carpet. Riders get no extra actions. Disabling a carpet drops it one square without fall damage; no remote shutdown. The crew can try to carry prisoners or remaining papers out aboard one, but must actually load and pilot it.

**Painted moon:** A main action adjacent rolls it up to 2 squares along the floor through a clear space, moving its cover with it. It cannot crush creatures; occupied squares stop it. Stamina 15, stability 2. It can block the central aisle or jam an opening by its actual footprint.

**Sea-serpent head:** Its hollow jaw is cover aboard carpet B, not a monster. A creature can spend a maneuver adjacent to open or shut the jaw; a shut jaw blocks line of effect through its mouth but does not lock or restrain anyone. Let a hero ride inside it, leap from it, or use it for a theatrical entrance.

**Wool bale:** A main action at the release drops it into its marked 2 × 2 footprint, dealing 6 damage and knocking creatures there prone. It remains blocking cover. Everyone can see and use it. These neutral props add no one-sided hazard EV.

**Round clock:** At the **end of round 5**, Belmonte abandons the plan to control the whole depot and orders retreat with whatever papers or captives his crew actually holds. He and the crew use ordinary turns and movement to get out. **Do not override a successful blockade. No new crew spawns.** If cornered, resolve the remaining fight, surrender, or a genuine bargain within the buffer; no automatic disappearance.

**End immediately when:** Belmonte is defeated, captured, or unconditionally surrenders—this **ends organized resistance immediately**, and his remaining crew releases captives and flees or surrenders. Also end when the heroes get every threatened civilian and their secured proof out of the encounter. Do not require exterminating minions. If the heroes retreat without everyone, retain the loss for follow-up; do not grant a free offscreen rescue.

**Hard encounter reward:** Award **2 Victories** for breaking the crew's hold and securing the release or evacuation of all civilians still threatened, with at least one usable record or cooperating witness preserved. Capturing Belmonte and freeing his captives qualifies; killing every guard does not. A genuinely decisive power or bargain that achieves the same objective earns the same reward. This does not create another planned combat.

### Rules at this battlefield

- **Initiative:** Heroes and enemy groups alternate turns; heroes choose their own order. Every creature acts once per round. A group resolves all its members together. No allies add combat turns or encounter strength; their help is off-map.
- **Malice:** Start this encounter with the party's average current Victories (round down). At the start of each round, add living heroes plus round number: normally +5, +6, +7, +8, +9. Discard unspent Malice after this encounter. Use only the printed abilities and Human Malice options below; there are no hidden reinforcements.
- **Potency and duration:** M/A/R/I/P refer to Might/Agility/Reason/Intuition/Presence; a condition after “M < 2,” for example, applies only if Might is below 2. EoT ends at the end of the target's next turn, or its current turn if imposed during that turn. Save ends: roll d10 at each turn's end; 6+ ends the effect.
- **Relevant conditions:** Taunted gives a double bane on ability rolls that do not target the taunter while they are within line of effect. Slowed caps speed at 2 and prevents shifting. Restrained sets speed to 0, prevents Stand Up and forced movement, gives a bane on ability rolls and Might/Agility tests, and grants abilities against the creature an edge; teleporting ends it. Prone gives a bane on strikes, grants melee strikes against the creature an edge, and makes crawling cost 1 extra square; Stand Up is a maneuver. Grabbed sets speed to 0, prevents Knockback and forced movement except by its source, and gives a bane on abilities not targeting the grabber. The source brings its captive when it moves; its speed is halved if the captive is at least its size. The grab ends if released, the target teleports, or forced movement separates the pair. Conditions do not stack with themselves.
- **Recovery:** Catch Breath is a maneuver: spend one of the hero's own Recoveries to regain their recovery value in Stamina. A dying hero cannot Catch Breath. There is no automatic healing between scenes.
- **Free strikes:** These monsters deal their listed fixed Free Strike damage, without a roll; range is their signature's range or melee 1/ranged 5, whichever is greater. An opportunity attack is a melee free strike when an adjacent enemy willingly moves away without shifting or teleporting. Forced movement does not provoke. A monster with a bane against that target cannot make the opportunity attack.
- **Positioning:** Cover exists when a solid object blocks at least half the target and imposes a bane on damage-dealing abilities against it. Difficult terrain costs 1 extra square to enter. Stability reduces horizontal forced movement by its value, minimum 0. Falling 2+ squares deals 2 damage per square and leaves the creature prone; reduce effective height by Agility, minimum 0. Use stairs and ladders at 1 extra square of movement per square climbed unless the creature has a climb speed.
- **Captures:** A hero reducing a creature to 0 Stamina can knock it unconscious rather than kill it. Do not require lethal damage to end an encounter. Do not attack downed heroes for convenience.

**Minions, fully inline:** Each color is a separate squad of four with its own pool. All members share one signature roll when attacking. Each target takes one attack result; a second or third minion on that same target adds its Free Strike damage (at most three attackers per target). Each minion has a move and a main action, a move and a maneuver, or two moves—not all three. A minion using a personal maneuver does not join the squad attack. A critical hit gives each participating minion a free strike.

Reduce a squad's pool by damage suffered. At every multiple of individual Stamina lost, remove one minion. A non-area hit can remove additional nearest squad members after the struck one; let the player narrate it. An area ability damages the pool once per affected minion, but cannot kill minions outside its area; cap that area damage at individual Stamina times affected members before immunity/weakness. Apply a squad's immunity or weakness only once to each damage source, not once per affected minion. Minions cannot regain or gain temporary Stamina and cannot be winded. Simultaneous free strikes from one squad into one target are one strike with summed damage.

Attach the named captain to its colored squad; keep the captain's Stamina separate. While that captain lives, the squad gains its printed With Captain bonus. A captain leads only one squad. A replacement non-minion can take command at the start of the next round. All squad members and their captain activate in the same initiative group.

**Human features below are book rules, not cult evidence.** Supernatural Insight ignores supernatural concealment; it does not read minds, detect lies, or defeat mundane cover. No monster gains additional unprinted abilities.

**Bloodstone Cuff — triggered action, self, Magic.** Trigger: Belmonte makes a power roll. He takes 5 unpreventable corruption damage to raise the outcome one tier, maximum tier 3. Once per round; this is his one triggered action that round.

**End Effect — trait.** At the end of his turn Belmonte may take 5 unpreventable damage to end one save-ends effect on himself. It cannot end a grab or damage already taken.

**Supernatural Insight — trait.** Ignore concealment granted by supernatural effects, not mundane concealment or cover.

**Villain actions:** Once per round, after another creature's turn, at no action or Malice cost. Use each of the following once per encounter, in numbered order. They are separate from his main and triggered actions.
1. **Clear the Gantry.** Up to four marksmen within 10 each make a ranged free strike. If a squad shoots one target simultaneously, sum the damage into one strike. No dead minions are replaced.
2. **Get Them Loaded.** Each ally within 10 shifts up to its speed. Until encounter end, Belmonte and his allies have damage immunity 2 while adjacent to another ally; do not add it to higher immunity of the same damage type.
3. **Make a Way.** Belmonte shifts up to 10 squares regardless of speed, then uses Hooked Baton and Enchanted Sabre against up to three targets. One adjacent ally per target may make a free strike against that target.

**Tactics:** Seize the clerk and records, destroy the bills if there is time, and leave before the disturbance brings help from the public street. Use pulls to expose heroes to marksmen and pushes to clear a route. Belmonte must use ordinary movement to escape; no teleport, invulnerability, or automatic off-map exit. Defeat, capture, or unconditional surrender of Belmonte immediately ends organized enemy resistance. Heroes evacuating the threatened civilians with their secured proof also ends the encounter; pursuing them is not a second prepared battle.

### Rewards and outcomes

- **Completed reporting commission — S + F:** **1 Victory** and **+1 Wealth to each hero**, once, independent of whether the original papers survived or Belmonte was captured. Copies and verifiable testimony suffice.
- **One finding only:** Half the monetary fee, expenses, and a follow-up lead; no investigation Victory or Wealth increase yet. Credit the installment if the missing finding is completed later.
- **Neither finding:** Agreed expenses only. Do not invent evidence or completion.
- **Hard encounter objective achieved:** **2 Victories**, separate from the reporting fee. Evaluate the rescue and preserved evidence from actual play, not the number of enemies killed.
- **Maximum:** **3 Victories and +1 Wealth per hero.** Record earned rewards at the table after play. No respite, automatic healing, XP conversion, character-file change, or app import during preparation.

> *Ottilie signs the payment papers.*
>
> *“We can show where the money went. And we can show what the captain did when someone asked about it.”*

Use that line only when both claims are supported. No publication bonus for inventing a bigger accusation.

### Leave them with a choice, not another errand list

The company paymaster, an intercepted receipt, or Anselmo's knowledge identifies **Civic Loomworks' office that sent the payoff**. If none survived, Ottilie's original payment record gives the registered company address. The next lead is always available, but the proof and witnesses the party controls determine how safely they can pursue it.

The immediate decision is whether the Font should **publish the captain's cover-up now** or **briefly hold the story while the heroes follow the company connection**. Explain the trade: publication puts witnesses and the captain's conduct on the public record but warns the company; delay preserves an investigative advantage but gives it time to move things. Ottilie may agree to a specific short delay, not indefinite suppression or a false story. Neither choice is a patron oath or a right answer.

Demona chooses whether to be quoted, advise privately, or stay out of the article. Dorian's personal history is not a condition of payment. The heroes have earned a relationship with the Font, not universal protection or certainty about Dury.
