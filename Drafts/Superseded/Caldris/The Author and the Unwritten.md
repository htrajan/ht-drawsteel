# The Author, Caldris, and the Unwritten

> **Tags:** INVENTED (Author, Caldris, cult, erasure, Concordance, interface) + CANON (Orden, Timescape, quintessence, psionics).
>
> **Use when:** Running erasure scenes, Caldran crossings, Unwritten cult activity, Demona's psionic reads, Dorian/Author confrontations, or M.A.C.'s Timescape thread.

## Director truth: two real worlds

The Author is a **living, embodied psionic person physically residing in Orden**. The operator has built or inherited an interface that reaches Caldris, uses changes there to prototype alterations to Orden, and presents those alterations to clients as erasure.

The Author sincerely believes:

1. Orden is the original and only fully legitimate world.
2. Caldris is a constructed testing environment.
3. Caldran people are sufficiently complex products of the environment, not moral equals.
4. Mastering the interface will permit a perfected Orden without disorder, error, political opposition, or unwanted witnesses.

The campaign's deeper truth is that **both worlds and their inhabitants are fully real**. Creation and reality are not opposites. It is not necessary to decide which world existed first, and no Act 2 evidence can prove primacy.

Caldris possesses:

- Complete quintessence rather than borrowed or projected essence.
- Nations, families, religions, scholarship, and political disputes unrelated to Orden.
- Recorded histories and physical strata that apparently predate the current interface.
- Native metaphysical systems, including the Concordance, that neither obey nor recognize the Author.

Orden possesses equally complete histories, relationships, and material consequence. The party's established past is not fabricated, and the Caldris mystery must never invalidate player-owned backstory.

---

## What the Author wants

The Author's project is to **perfect Orden**.

1. **Prototype revisions in Caldris.** Model a desired absence or replacement through Caldran metaphysics, then impose the result on Orden.
2. **Remove resistant people.** Sell erasure to clients such as Minister Dury while refining the interface on politically convenient targets.
3. **Stabilize the result.** Use witnesses, written records, relays, and civic cover stories to keep Orden from rejecting the edit.
4. **Expand access.** Establish more thin places and relays so revisions can be performed without the operator's physical presence at each site.
5. **Resolve the Caldran anomaly.** The Author regards Caldris's apparent pre-interface history and autonomous safeguards as emergent errors, not evidence against the premise.

The title **Author** is a claim of ownership, not proof of creation.

## Cult: the Unwritten

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Public face** | None; cells hide as archivist societies, salons, restoration clubs, and civic contractors |
| **Doctrine** | Orden is a flawed draft. The Author can test corrections in a blank margin and return them perfected. The erased are not murdered; they are removed from a faulty version. |
| **Structure** | Decentralized **Curators** such as Vharos Kell; higher ranks use titles including **Editors** and **Prologues** |
| **Recruitment** | Disillusioned scholars, clerks, artists, and officials attracted to clean, absolute solutions |
| **Patron pipeline** | Powerful clients supply names, access, money, and cover stories without necessarily seeing Caldris or meeting the Author |

**Session 1 cell:** Vharos Kell operated from the Levitating Carpet Spooling Factory. The factory's warp threads, vertical looms, and civic isolation made it useful as an interface anchor.

**After Session 1:** Kell and the defeated cell are dead in ash. The party is pulled through the interface during the ash transfer. Other cells, relays, and clients remain.

### Redactors — deferred Orden cleanup assets

A **Redactor** is a single-purpose cleanup asset used when clients need records destroyed but full erasure would be too costly or visible. It appears as a person-shaped absence wrapped in self-revising paper.

- It destroys written names, records, and fragments of recent recollection.
- It cannot perform full erasure, create a relay, or speak with the Author's authority.
- Spoken names and sonic force disrupt its paper body.
- Destroying one reveals nothing about the Author; its shell falls blank.

The Redactor and Blue-Wax contractors remain available for the deferred **Ash Ledger** investigation. They do not appear during the Caldris visit in Session 2.

## Minister Corvane Dury's connection

Dury is **not** the Author. He is a client and political enabler.

Years ago, while rising through the Ministry of Revels, he found ledger ghosts—people paid twice and then remembered by almost no one. He followed those discrepancies to an Unwritten salon offering discretion beyond murder.

His bargain was simple: remove enemies and mistakes; he would provide permits, guards, money, and official explanations. He has never needed to accept Caldrans as people to benefit from the result.

**Public layer:** Beloved minister of festivals and civic works.

**Party layer after Session 1:** Kell named Dury as the official Vesk Aldermere was about to expose.

**Director layer:** Dury submits requests through Curator channels. He may know the Author is physically in Orden, but he does not know the operator's identity or location.

The deferred Ash Ledger chain remains:

**Factory accounts → Ministry beautification money → Lantern Relief Account → Sabine's memorandum → Comptroller Halren Pike → physical interface infrastructure**

Do not reveal Pike or the memorandum's contents in the Caldris session.

## Rules of erasure

These rules remain stable across the campaign.

### Orden-side effect

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | The target dissolves from the edges inward into gray ash; a scream often cuts off mid-sound. |
| **Memory** | Unshielded witnesses forget the target over days unless they wrote the name before the erasure. Demona and strong-willed witnesses can resist the fuzzing. |
| **Evidence** | Carried objects can remain. Ash remains briefly in Orden unless the interface is open to receive it. |
| **Full focus** | A completed, direct revision such as Vesk's cannot be stopped with a reactive test. Preventing one requires disrupting the interface, relay, or target conditions beforehand. |
| **Incomplete ritual** | Local cult techniques such as Kell's Strike the Scene inflict psychic harm and conditions but cannot erase a hero. |

### What happens to the ash

When the interface is open, ash crosses into Caldris as **causally blank grayfall**. Caldrans use grayfall for ceramics, pigment, soil, medicine, and construction.

The campaign does not establish whether grayfall contains:

- The original person.
- A partial psychic impression.
- A copy produced by the interface.
- Only transformed matter that can influence later Caldran life.

Caldran people are not generally made from recent Orden victims, and no Caldran is less real because grayfall contributed to their environment.

### Why memory changes in Orden

Orden's memory fuzz is not proof that Orden is artificial. It is the subtle Orden expression of the same causal rules that are visible in Caldris: a revision seeks a consistent history. Written names and active witnesses provide resistance because they remain attached to the prior state.

### Psionic tells

- A living mind rather than a repeating spell.
- Cold stage-dust odor near an active interface.
- A hollow white theatrical mask imposed by the Author's chosen persona.
- Text or geometry pulling toward a missing center.
- A resonance thread leading toward relays or an open interface for minutes, not hours.

The coldness and scale of early contact led Demona to perceive the Author as a distant intelligence. Session 2 reframes that read: the signal is heavily amplified, but its source breathes, pulses, and becomes distracted like a living body.

## Relays and interfaces

### Relays

A relay is a living Orden person converted into an antenna for a remote revision.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Creation** | The Author or a trained Curator primes a susceptible mind, often without informed consent. |
| **Behavior** | The relay moves with imposed precision and is drawn toward extraction or an interface anchor. |
| **End** | When the connection severs, the operator can erase the relay to remove evidence, as with Pell Quist. |
| **Demona's read** | Psionic examination can distinguish the victim from the controlling signal. |
| **Network** | Multiple citywide relays remain an Act 2 thread. |

### Thin places and interfaces

Stages, archives, factories, and rehearsal halls work well because they already hold competing versions: drafts, performances, records, and manufactured patterns.

Known anchors:

- The Gilded Lantern stage beneath the Grand Amphitheater—the strongest Capital anchor and site of Dorian's audience erasure.
- The Levitating Carpet Spooling Factory—Kell's temporary interface and the party's first crossing.
- Unidentified civic infrastructure paid through Dury's financial chain—the route to the Author's physical Orden operation.

A tear is not a hole into blank nothing. It is a temporary interface between Orden and Caldris. Closing one breaks that local connection without injuring either world.

## Caldran metaphysics

Caldris is materially ordinary to its inhabitants. Its unusual rules are civic facts rather than constant spectacles.

### Consensus stabilizes access

Multiple versions of a place can coexist. Shared observation determines which version is easiest for public movement without proving the others false. Homes, traditions, or private routes can remain attached to a minority history.

### Contradictions create traversable branches

Two incompatible accounts can become different doors, stairs, rooms, or districts. Resolving a dispute changes access and emphasis, not necessarily the past itself.

### Major changes need an unchanged witness

A broad revision needs at least one observer or reference that remains outside the change. That stable viewpoint defines the difference between before and after.

This is why the Gilded Lantern audience revision left Dorian standing. The Author deliberately used the performer in the stage light as the unchanged witness. The operator did not plan Dorian's murder by the bereaved or his return as a revenant.

### Relationships create causal standing

People belong in Caldris because their lives are attached to other lives and prior events. Visitors can gradually gain temporary protection as Caldrans witness, remember, and rely on them.

Permanent Caldran residency does **not** require false memories. It requires surrendering the Orden tether and allowing a continuing Caldran future to become the person's operative causal home.

## The Concordance

The Concordance is intrinsic to Caldris and apparently older than the Author's present interface.

It detects **orphaned causality**: consequences whose causes have no Caldran history. It does not identify foreign people by ancestry, accent, clothing, morality, or resident suspicion.

### Detection sequence

1. Reflections, tickets, footprints, or registries fail to attach the visitors to a prior route.
2. White Index Marks trace the consequences they have caused.
3. Indexing bodies attempt to remove those consequences and locate the foreign causes.
4. The White Cartographer manifests, confirms the classification, and attempts deletion.

The Concordance initially seeks **erasure**, not quarantine, assimilation, or expulsion. It cannot verify Orden as a valid destination and therefore cannot safely “send the heroes home.”

It ignores Caldran bystanders because they are accounted for. Helping a foreigner does not instantly make a Caldran foreign, though repeated aid can draw later scrutiny.

### White Cartographer

The White Cartographer is intelligent enough to reason and answer questions but has the priorities of a consistency mechanism. It is not cruel, theatrical, or secretly allied with the party.

Destroying one body interrupts the current deletion attempt. It does not kill the Concordance. Later sessions can explore disguising causal traces, negotiating a valid status, or changing how the Cartographer classifies the party.

## Connection to Dorian

- The Gilded Lantern audience was the Author's first known Capital mass revision.
- Dorian remained because the revision required an unchanged witness with a view of the targeted audience.
- He was not erased, rejected, resurrected, or accidentally outside a blast radius.
- The bereaved found the one living person amid the ash, blamed him, and murdered him.
- His revenant return was not part of the Author's design. It turned the retained reference into a persistent, remembering threat.
- The Session 1 playbill is Capital's false accusation made physical, not an Author bookmark.

The cult may still speak incorrectly of Dorian as someone who “wouldn't stay unwritten.” Kell knew he was the retained witness but interpreted every fact through cult doctrine.

Session 2 reveals **why Dorian remained**. It does not identify who commissioned the Gilded Lantern revision, expose the Author, name every killer, or resolve Dorian's public exoneration.

## M.A.C. and Timescape implications

M.A.C. can establish in Session 2 that Caldris has complete quintessence. This is a major clue, not proof of primacy.

| Layer | Available conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| **Public Orden theory** | Manifolds and planes can have different physical rules; most scholars have no model for Caldris. |
| **Party after Session 2** | Orden and Caldris are two complete realities joined by an engineered psionic interface. The operator lives in Orden. |
| **Director** | Both worlds are fully real. The Author's claim of construction does not confer moral or metaphysical ownership. |

Avoid comparing Caldris to Hell, the World Below, an afterlife, a dream, or the Abyssal Waste as though any analogy explains it.

## Act 2 trajectory and permanent residency

### Early Act 2

- The party survives a short first visit because the Orden connection extracts them before deletion completes.
- Later crossings require temporary causal anchors created through Caldran relationships, witnessed deeds, and prepared routes.
- Orden political investigation exposes the money and infrastructure supporting the physical interface.

### Act 2 climax

Repeated revisions destabilize the connection. Shutting down the Author's infrastructure requires permanently severing travel between the worlds.

Before severance, the party collectively chooses its continuing home:

- **Remain in Orden:** Return and cut the interface from the Orden side. Caldris continues beyond contact.
- **Remain in Caldris:** Cross, surrender the Orden tether, and become causally recognized through genuine Caldran history. Orden continues beyond contact.

A hero who chooses the other world receives an epilogue and retires from the active party. Neither reality collapses, and the choice never reveals a “correct” real world.

The interface must be neutralized before this choice so either branch denies the Author further editing access.

## Calling cards and signatures

Use sparingly and distinguish their sources:

### Author and cult

- Smooth white half-mask.
- Yellowed playbill with Dorian's name left legible.
- Perfect calligraphy and theatrical editing language.
- Cold stage-dust smell.
- Gray ash moving toward an open interface.

### Concordance

- Hair-thin white survey lines.
- Measurements without units.
- Reflections or records missing the visitors' origin point.
- Actions reversing before bodies are targeted.
- Blank geometric bodies that ignore accounted-for residents.

The White Cartographer does not use masks, playbills, monologues, or the Author's literary vocabulary.

## Pitfalls for future agents

- Do not call Caldris an illusion, simulation, afterlife, void, dream, or disposable draft in Director narration.
- Do not let the Author's belief settle the reality question.
- Do not portray Caldrans primarily as erased Orden people or echoes of familiar NPCs.
- Do not introduce alternate copies of the PCs or their loved ones without explicit approval.
- Do not make everyday Caldrans detect Orden visitors by sight; the Concordance detects missing causal history.
- Do not make the Concordance serve the Author.
- Do not turn the White Cartographer into a theatrical villain or lore lecturer.
- Do not retcon player-owned history or imply the heroes' Orden lives were fabricated.
- Do not resolve which world came first. The Director truth is that both are real now.
- Keep full-power erasure consistent and preventable only by disrupting its prerequisites before completion.
- Keep Dury politically protected until the party has evidence and standing.
