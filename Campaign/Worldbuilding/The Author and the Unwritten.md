## What the heroes have encountered

- Vesk Aldermere was publicly unmade into ash at the Grand Amphitheater.
- Pell Quist was a relay/victim in the investigation, not the ultimate perpetrator; use the actual chase outcome for what the heroes perceived.
- The Levitating Carpet Spooling Factory housed Vharos Kell's Unwritten cell and a tear used in its confrontation.
- Kell is dead in ash. His obscure cell's defeat did not create a civic power vacuum.
- The party has heard of the Author at a high, vague level. Preserve Kell's words if spoken; his confidence does not make his doctrine cosmological fact.
- Dorian recognizes a connection between this erasure and his vanished audience. Why he survived remains unanswered.

The exact words of an optional speech, a prop not collected, and a private revelation not shared must not silently become party knowledge. Consult [Narrative](../Narrative.md) and the table record.

## Active preparation: political cover and diverted repair money

**Director only:** Bellafonte → Rudiger Fenwick → Civic Loomworks and Festooning → Baldo Merovanni → Dury. The heroes can expose the commissioner's false approvals, the broker's gifts and the contractor's invoices. The last two links are later investigation, not names conveniently present in a low-level broker's complete confession.

Vesk's request for repeated complaints about completed public repairs is newly prepared evidence. It leads toward the Dog-leg, where a pump was never replaced despite an official completion report. Some repair money instead funded affluent clients' improvements and earlier factory supplies. Bellafonte knowingly approves the fraud; this does not prove he understood the Unwritten or arranged the supernatural murder.

Civic Loomworks' new boarding-up order is dated the morning after the confrontation, following a report of damage. No contractor guard post existed at the heroes' Session 1 arrival. Commissioner Bellafonte meets Fenwick in his basement collection and attempts to use its security constructs against discovered investigators. Those constructs are ordinary campaign-created magical automata, not proof of an erasure mechanism.

## Erasure and relays: continuity limits

Keep the sensory facts already played: ash, interrupted speech, the erasure signature, psionic disturbance, and any actual observations of the relay. Do not replace that history with a silent disappearance.

Earlier preparation proposed universal memory decay, fixed interface physics, and an unchanged-witness requirement. None should be treated as a revealed law merely because it appeared in a draft. Use a memory effect only to the degree established in play; do not erase a hero's established recollection to protect the mystery.

No full erasure occurs in Session 2. Bellafonte's collection animates through a visible control and has explicit combat rules. Clever prevention works; there is no invisible force that guarantees his escape. Corbin's dismissal and the office's closure are mundane institutional acts, not new supernatural powers.

A tear seen during Session 1 establishes a phenomenon, not a complete map of where it leads. If the heroes closed it, it stays closed. There is no new crossing or compelled return.

## Safeguards for future writing

- Preserve player-owned history, including Demona's unresolved disgrace and Dorian's wrongful murder.
- Distinguish an NPC's belief, a proposed explanation, and a fact learned through play.
- Give future discoveries observable evidence; do not explain hidden anatomy, intent, or cosmology in read-aloud.
- Do not make all seven powers cult accomplices or give Dury authority over every house and guild.
- Let evidence and patronage change what actions are possible; do not give Dury invulnerability by fiat.
- Record the actual outcome of the upcoming session only after it has been played.
