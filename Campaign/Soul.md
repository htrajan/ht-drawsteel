# Overview

Treat this as a document of meta-rules on how to build this campaign: best practices on designing encounters, narrative beats, exploration montages, puzzles, and more. Append to this document to encode into memory any new rules and best practices we discover along the way.

## Pacing Player Requests

Trying to pander to every player's request in a single session will come across as forced and unnatural. Aim to deliberately serve only 1-2 player requests per session, chosen for what fits the story being told, and let fulfillment of the others happen naturally and incidentally over time rather than being engineered into every session.

## Opening a Session / Act

- **Start with a Bang, scaled to scope.** An act opener's inciting vignette needs gravitas proportional to the act — a citywide, high-stakes, public event, not a small personal errand. Reserve small, intimate hooks for later once stakes are established.
- **Start in the action; deliver backstory as flashback.** Open at or near the dramatic event and drop exposition (how the party got here, arrival logistics) as a flashback or in-scene texture afterward, rather than front-loading travel/setup.
- **Defer big personal payoffs until the party has earned stature.** Plant a hero's origin/backstory as an active, escalating mystery early, but hold the real resolution until the heroes have the standing (social, political, level) to make it land. Early sessions prove the thread is alive and personal; they don't close it.

## Prose & Read-Aloud Style

- **Avoid on-the-nose literalisms that instruct the players how to think or feel.** Don't spell out a scene's meaning, stakes, or a character's emotional takeaway ("he is the loose end of what just happened," "this is the villain you should hate," "you feel horrified"). Describe the concrete sensory facts — what is seen, heard, felt physically — and trust the players to draw the conclusion and supply the emotion. Show, don't tell.
- **Keep dramatic intensity around ~7.5/10, not maxed out.** Read-aloud and NPC dialogue should be evocative but restrained. Trim melodramatic tics — "Gods help you," ALL-CAPS words for emphasis, stacked triplets of adjectives, "the night that broke your life in two," portentous "…and it has been waiting" closers. Let the events carry the weight; over-purple prose reads as trying too hard.

## Structuring Skill Challenges & Branches

- **Fail forward — converge branches on the same required lead.** When a montage test or skill challenge gates progress, design every outcome (total success / partial / total failure) to still deliver the one piece of information or access the plot needs, varying only the cost, extra intel, or complication. The session must never dead-end on a failed roll.
- **Check that a montage's partial-success tier is actually reachable.** The book's partial-success rule ("hit the failure limit but with ≥2 more successes than failures") can only trigger when the **success limit exceeds the failure limit by 3+** (e.g., a Hard 6/2 spread). At tighter spreads (e.g., Moderate 5/3, Easy 4/4) that margin can never coincide with hitting the failure limit, so partial success becomes mathematically impossible. When you want three distinct outcomes at a tight spread, judge the middle tier by **raw successes banked when the test ends** instead (e.g., "3rd failure with ≥2 successes = partial"), and say so explicitly.

## Encounter Building (Draw Steel reference)

- **Party encounter strength (ES):** each hero = 4 + 2×level; sum for the party. (Four level-3 heroes = ES 40.) Add a "virtual hero" of ES for every 2 average Victories.
- **Difficulty by budget:** Standard = ES to ES + one hero. Hard = above standard up to ES + three heroes (worth 2 Victories). Aim climactic/session-ending fights at **hard**.
- **Creature level cap:** no creature more than 2 levels above the heroes (3 if they have 6+ Victories). This is why level-6 book monsters (e.g., Voiceless Talkers) are off-limits for a level-3 party — reskin/downscale into custom lower-level creatures instead.
- **Star of the show:** for a villain meant to last the whole fight, run a hard encounter and make them a Leader/Solo whose EV is ≥ 1/3 of the budget; the fight ends when they fall.
- **Crowding rules:** ≤ 8 creatures per hero; if > 3 per hero, at least half must be minions; ≤ 6 distinct stat blocks. Minion squads exist to be scythed by AoE (serves Keth).
- **Tactical maps:** build in verticality, cover, chokepoints, hazards, and moving/interactive terrain (serves Miran's request and rewards positioning).

## Run-Sheet Standard for Session Docs

When asked to produce a run-ready session doc, make it fully self-contained so no other book is needed at the table: read-aloud boxed text for each scene, NPC bios with motivation + pitfalls, full montage-test parameters, and complete inline stat blocks (reprint reflavored bestiary creatures rather than cross-referencing).

**Colocate rules with the scene that uses them; do not write a general "rules used" section.** Explain each mechanic (montage/test-difficulty tiers, combat conditions, Malice, villain actions, etc.) inline within the scene where it comes up, repeating it in another scene if that scene also needs it. Skip absolute basics that don't need restating (what a power roll is, what the five characteristics are); document only the specific rules being applied.
