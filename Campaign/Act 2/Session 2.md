## Scene 1 — Corbin's invitation (0:00–0:15)

### Breakfast first

Use the established tavern and keeper. The heroes returned exhausted and slept; no factory replay or respite.

> *Breakfast arrives to the sound of an argument downstairs. Someone wants yesterday's price for today's bread. The keeper sets a pot between you, checks the doorway, and takes a folded note from an apron pocket.*
>
> *“This came for you this morning. The runner said to let you sleep.”*

Let them eat, react and read the note:

> *“I'd like to speak privately about Vesk Aldermere. Send a reply with the keeper. You choose the place. Please don't come to my office yet. — Melvin Corbin”*

Meet only after their reply reaches him. If they decline, send the recruitment notice with a second note:

> *“My office is taking on civilian help, but refusing further statements about Vesk's death. Lieutenant Petrovic handles applicants. You can ask her about the work without me. — Corbin”*

### Once they have privacy

> *Corbin sets his notebook on the table but leaves it closed.*
>
> *“My superintendent told me to stop questioning people about Vesk. He wants her listed as missing and the investigation closed. Not murdered. Missing.”*
>
> *“You were there. Have you learned anything since we spoke?”*

Let them answer. He does not already know about the factory, Kell, or their discoveries.

### Why Corbin suggests deputy work

Give these beats as part of this private conversation, with room for questions between them. They are the explanation for the offer, not optional lore behind a successful test.

**The work he can offer:**

> *“There is a way to get you inside the office. We're paying civilians to help with complaints and patrols. It isn't detective work, but you'd be able to talk to the clerks and read the open reports.”*
>
> *“The Prince died three years ago. Forty-one years old, and no heir. Four houses and three guilds want to decide who rules next. People pick a side. Lately, more of them have been picking fights.”*
>
> *“Yesterday a patrol spent the afternoon breaking up a fight at the market. They were supposed to be following up thefts. Those reports are still waiting.”*

**Why the office cannot simply add guards:**

> *He takes a metal seal from its cord and turns the stamped dragon toward them.*
>
> *“The Prince's seal. Every sworn guard carries one. The Prince used to approve more each year. That stopped when he died. We can fill an empty post, but we can't just add another patrol.”*
>
> *“That's why we're taking on civilian help. Hear the complaints. Help people settle a quarrel before it becomes another fight. Bring back what needs an officer, with enough detail that we can act.”*

**Why he is asking them:**

> *“You could do some good. And while you're there, ask about Vesk. See whether they'll take your statement.”*
>
> *“Lieutenant Petrovic handles the applicants. She'll tell you what the work allows. I can't promise access to the murder file.”*

**Responses to likely questions:**

- **“Is Vesk's case just waiting like the theft reports?”**

  > *“No. I've been told not to take more statements. Not to wait until there's time. To stop. That's not how we handle a case we're too busy to finish.”*

- **“Why not hire more private guards?”**

  > *“Their employer pays them to guard his property. Their charter tells them what they're allowed to do. That doesn't make them our officers. We need someone to hear the complaints outside those guarded doorways.”*

- **“Who else could help?”**

  > *“The Gold Buttons enforce the law here on the Stays. Other districts have their own guards. Vorona's Far Mariners are the closest thing Capital has to a citywide force, but they don't command us. I can't order them to take over our cases.”*

- **“Did the houses kill the Prince?”**

  > *“I don't know. Nobody's brought me proof.”*

**Director:** The staffing problem is real; it does not excuse the cover-up. These specific disturbances and the deputy program are campaign inventions; source distinctions are at the top of the sheet. Keep the full breakfast and private conversation inside fifteen minutes.

## Scene 2 — A place inside the door (0:15–0:30)

### At the Peacekeepers' office

**Location:** Ledger Lane Peacekeepers' Office, a campaign-created Gold Buttons complaints and investigations office on the Stays—not an all-Capital police headquarters.

>
> *Behind the counter, a uniformed officer listens to a fish seller and a carter blame each other for a ruined load. Her gold buttons are polished; one cuff has been repaired with the wrong-colored thread.*
>
> *“I can hear both of you,” she says. “I can't hear both of you at once.”*

**Lieutenant Hanae Petrovic:** Human Gold Button; brisk, fair, short of people. Wants useful help; will not grant powers she lacks. Not part of the cover-up.

### Let the heroes see the pressure

A brief interruption, not another assignment or combat:

> *Two guards bring in a man with a split lip and another clutching a torn sash. One guard sets a broken bottle on the counter.*
>
> *“At the ferry again, Lieutenant. Started over whose duke ought to be prince.”*

> *The woman with the window frame turns around. “And finished in my shop.”*

The men are partisans, not house soldiers. If asked who ordered the fight, the guard answers:

> *“I saw these two swinging bottles. I didn't see anyone giving orders.”*

Keep this a visible example, not a second explanation of the succession. The whole office scene has fifteen minutes.

### What “citizen deputy” actually grants

Hanae offers **signed work letters**. “Deputy” is a local nickname for this campaign arrangement, not a newly discovered canonical rank.

> *“Here about the work? Good. Tell me your names, and what you're willing to take on.”*

Let the heroes describe their skills and limits. Then she lays an unsigned letter on the counter:

> *“This says you're helping this office. Show it when you ask questions. You'll be able to read the open complaints and request public work records for the job you've chosen. When you come back, ask for the duty clerk; you won't have to join this queue again.”*
>
> *“Listen to people who want to speak to you. Bring us their accounts and anything you find. If someone needs arresting, call an officer.”*
>
> *“You cannot search someone's house because they don't like your questions. This isn't a warrant, and it doesn't make you a knight.”*
>
> *“Anything in those terms you can't agree to?”*

**If they ask how far the letter reaches:**

> *“Our work is on the Stays. Another district's guard doesn't answer to us. This won't get you into sealed files, change what weapons you're permitted to carry, or excuse breaking the law.”*

**If they want work without joining:**

> *“You don't have to swear allegiance to anyone. If you don't want the letter, the notices and public records are still there. You'll have to wait your turn at the counter.”*

Declining loses faster access, not the adventure. Do not later revoke letters they never accepted.

**Tell the players:** The full reward is **+1 Wealth per hero**, not one point divided between them. Write down the offer.

**Director:** These reward terms are campaign inventions. Cancellation of future work does not cancel an earned claim; Hanae can arrange payment outside later. Partial findings earn wages, not fractional Wealth. No patron commitment or second negotiation.

## Scene 5 — Find the house, find the meeting (1:05–1:25)

### The open question

> *Outside, the porter stops to tighten the rope around his crate. Bellafonte's carriage waits by the curb. Behind the office window, the clerk puts the petitions away.*
>
> *Where do you start?*

**Goal:** Locate the house, learn where and when private business happens, and choose a way to approach it. Do not announce those answers or require one predetermined sequence. Spend roughly twelve minutes on the hunt and eight on reconnaissance and the meeting.

**Fixed facts, Director only:** The house is **18 Copper Beech Walk, Foxes**; a campaign-created address. Fenwick visits this afternoon with the payment papers. Their meeting is in the basement gallery; the steward admits workers through the rear stairs. The gallery's security is already running. These facts stay put whichever route the heroes choose.

The following are responses to player plans, **not four compulsory stations**. One sustained approach can locate the house and establish an entry; another can improve their position. Let them split up, combine methods, or invent a better one.

### Follow someone

**Follow Bellafonte's carriage or the porter; distract them, keep pace over the roofs, or use a suitable ability.**

> *The carriage leaves the shopfronts behind. Ahead, a water cart is spraying the paving outside a row of walled gardens. Bellafonte gets out beneath the number eighteen on Copper Beech Walk. A servant takes his coat.*
>
> *The porter passes the front gate and turns down the lane beside the garden wall.*

Following the porter to the rear reveals the delivery stairs. Watching the house afterward shows the private visitor:

> *A man carrying a slim leather case knocks twice at the rear doors. The steward greets him.*
>
> *“Mr. Fenwick. He's downstairs.”*

**Risk:** A conspicuous tail alerts Bellafonte; the party still reaches the house, but the steward refuses an unescorted visit. Discreet tracking can earn a rear approach or a lightwell position. No automatic discovery of a successful tail.

### Work the delivery route

**Talk to the porter, offer useful help, examine a delivery label, or ask Terenio about tradespeople who serve wealthy collectors.**

> *The porter eases the crate onto the curb.*
>
> *“Cabinetmaker's delivery. Bellafonte wants it in his collection before his visitor arrives. Give me a hand down the stairs and I'll split the carrying fee.”*

> *“Eighteen Copper Beech Walk, in Foxes. Rear doors. It's all downstairs.”*

> *The cabinetmaker checks the delivery book.*
>

The restorer can explain the gallery controls using the dialogue below. **Risk:** A poor deception means an escort, not a disappearing address. Genuine assistance needs no roll. Workers know their jobs, not the fraud.

**Request property records, trace an official's declared residence, or ask an appropriate clerk to help locate the property.** This is a player-chosen use of the deputy access, not an automatic address handed out with the complaints. The record supplies the house, **not the meeting or an entry plan**. At the address, let them decide whether to watch arrivals, speak to staff, or examine the building:

### Make a social approach

### Controls, terrain and escape — complete scenario rules

**Corner stop mechanisms — maneuver, no test:** Four wall-mounted controls sit at **B2, S2, B15 and S15**, each adjacent to a usable floor square. A hero adjacent to a ready control spends a **maneuver** to activate it. No resource, key, skill roll, main action, line of sight to the targets or sustained interaction is required. Color assignment stays fixed for the encounter even if minion captains change. The target group is fixed by the color and matching symbol; the stop reaches every surviving member anywhere in the gallery. It never affects the Marshal, either fugitive, a hero or an innocent worker.

**Duration:** Pause the affected non-leader group immediately **until the end of that group's next scheduled activation**. Its next activation is still selected in the usual alternating order, but paused members do nothing. Then they restart. If the group already acted this round, the pause lasts through its activation next round. The red group's Marshal takes its full turn normally even while the red attendants are paused.

**While paused:** Creatures cannot take actions, maneuvers, voluntary movement, free strikes or triggered actions, including ones granted by the Marshal. Their command benefits, With Captain benefits and defensive auras are suspended. Existing durations and saves still resolve on their scheduled turns; no healing, condition removal or reset. They remain solid, targetable occupants, can take damage and forced movement normally, and retain their ordinary Stamina and stability. No automatic criticals or extra damage. Minion pools remain separate and unchanged. A paused captain is not dead; its benefits return on restart if it survives.

**Recovery cycle:** A used control cannot be used again until its group has completed **one later, unpaused activation** after the skipped one. The dial shows **green = ready; white = paused; amber = recovering**. Multiple heroes cannot extend the same pause by repeatedly pulling a handle. Each control is independent: different groups can be paused at once, but none can be locked down continuously. An amber group gets its full working activation even if the party camps beside its control.

**Example:** Blue is stopped before its round-1 activation. Blue skips round 1, restarts at that activation's end, then acts normally in round 2; its control becomes ready at the end of blue's round-2 activation. If stopped after it acted in round 1, it skips round 2, acts in round 3, and becomes ready at the end of that round-3 activation. Neither timing pauses the leader or creates an extra activation.

**Physical interaction:** A control is a size-1T object, Stamina 20, no immunity or weakness. Its spring-loaded handle cannot be held or jammed for a longer stop: the receiver restarts from the Marshal's charge. Explain this before an attempted alteration. Destroying a ready control releases one normal pause, then leaves that control unusable; destroying a paused or recovering control does not extend or repeat its effect. Ordinary powers still affect constructs under their own rules; the corner-stop exception is not blanket immunity for the leader.

No additional protection against a hero's applicable suppression, sabotage or power. If an ability can shut down this magical construct under its actual rules, apply it; don't invent immunity. Its other actions still work if an effect suppresses only one named feature.

**Displays:** A hero adjacent to a case section can tip it with a main action. An attendant caught in the line counts separately for area damage. A PC can break an ordinary case to take a small evidence page without an additional theft puzzle.

### Core combat rules used here

- Heroes and enemy groups alternate; every creature acts once per round. Non-minions normally have a move, maneuver and main action, and one triggered action per round. A main action can buy another move or maneuver.
- **Malice:** Start with average current Victories, rounded down; add living heroes plus round number at each round's start (normally +5/+6/+7/+8/+9). Spend only on options printed below. No separate hidden faction Malice list.
- **Power rolls:** 2d10 + listed modifier; ≤11 / 12–16 / 17+ are tiers 1/2/3. A natural 19–20 makes an ability tier 3 and grants an additional main action. Edge adds 2, bane subtracts 2; two edges/banes instead raise/lower outcome one tier. Edges and banes cancel before applying.
- **Potency:** M/A/R/I/P mean characteristics. “M < 2” applies its effect only below Might 2. **EoT** ends at the end of the affected creature's next turn, or current turn if applied during it. **Save ends:** d10 at each turn's end; 6+ ends the effect.
- **Prone:** Bane on strikes; melee strikes against you gain an edge; crawling costs an extra square. Stand Up is a maneuver. **Slowed:** Speed 2; cannot shift. **Grabbed:** Speed 0, cannot Knockback or be forcibly moved except by the grabber; bane on abilities not targeting the grabber. Movement separating the pair or teleporting ends the grab. The grabber moves at half speed if dragging a creature its size or larger.
- **Grab:** Maneuver, Melee/Weapon, melee 1, one creature. Roll Might: ≤11 no effect; 12–16 grab, but the target may first make a melee free strike; 17+ grabbed. Normally target your size or smaller; Might 2+ permits targets up to a size equal to Might. Normally hold only one creature. The two fugitives have no free strike, so tier 2 still captures them without damage.
- **Escape Grab:** Maneuver, self; roll Might or Agility, with a bane if smaller than the grabber: ≤11 no effect; 12–16 escape but the grabber may first make a melee free strike; 17+ escape cleanly. Fugitives use their printed characteristics and this action, not automatic escapes.
- **Recoveries:** Catch Breath is a maneuver, spending one of the hero's own Recoveries to regain recovery value; a dying hero cannot use it in combat. Outside combat, heroes may spend their remaining Recoveries normally. No free healing between scenes.
- **Free strikes:** These monsters deal listed damage without a roll; melee 1/ranged 5 or their signature distance, whichever is greater. An adjacent enemy willingly moving away without shifting/teleporting provokes a melee opportunity attack; forced movement does not. A monster with a bane against that target cannot make that opportunity attack.
- **Terrain:** Cover imposes a bane on damaging abilities when an intervening solid object blocks at least half a target. Difficult terrain costs +1 movement. Stability reduces horizontal forced movement, minimum 0. A fall of 2+ squares deals 2 damage per square and leaves the creature prone; reduce effective height by Agility. Ordinary climbing costs +1 movement per square unless a climb speed applies.
- Reducing an NPC to 0 can knock them unconscious rather than kill them. Deactivated automata stay down; no second phase.

**Minions inline:** Four separate squads, each **32 Stamina** (four × 8). A squad shares one attack roll; each target takes one result, plus Free Strike damage for each extra attacker, maximum three minions per target. A minion takes a move and main action, move and maneuver, or two moves—not all three. A squad critical gives each participating minion another main action, resolved together under the same squad rules.

Damage reduces the squad pool; remove a minion for every 8 lost. Non-area overflow removes additional nearest squadmates. Area damage is applied per member in the area, capped at 8 per affected member before immunity/weakness; ordinarily no casualties outside the area. Apply immunity/weakness once per source to the pool, last; this can change the number removed. Minions cannot regain Stamina, gain temporary Stamina or become winded. Simultaneous free strikes into one target combine into one strike.

Each squad has at most one named non-minion captain, whose Stamina is separate. Red's captain is the Marshal; blue's is the armor; green's is its first lion; yellow's is its lion if present. Captain and squad share an initiative group. **With Captain** ends when that captain falls; a surviving non-minion can assume command at the start of the next round.
